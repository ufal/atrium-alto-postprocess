## What PR #32 changed

The merged calibration introduces six principal behavioral changes:

1. **Reference tokens no longer lower the valid-word ratio.**
   Dates, citations, initials, sigla, units, dotted abbreviations, figure references, and pure punctuation are excluded from both the numerator and denominator of `compute_valid_ratio()`. For example, `24.2.2020`, `/1933/32`, `I.L.`, `Mzm.`, `t. III`, and `Obr.3` are treated as neutral rather than invalid words. ([github.com](https://github.com/ufal/atrium-alto-postprocess/pull/32))

2. **One- and two-token lines now use a structural gate.**
   The new `rule_short_line` runs before the previous short-garbage route. FastText confidence is intentionally ignored for these lines because the language identifier is considered unreliable on very short input. Structurally clean short tokens can therefore become `Clear` even when a gibberish detector fires. ([raw.githubusercontent.com](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ufal/atrium-alto-postprocess/test/text_util.py))

3. **Character-level damage caps output at `Noisy`.**
   The new detector catches internal OCR damage such as stray symbols, apostrophes between letters, lowercase letter/digit mixtures, vowel-less lowercase runs, and suspicious mixed case. Its design constraint is that damage prevents `Clear` but does not itself force `Trash`. ([github.com](https://github.com/ufal/atrium-alto-postprocess/pull/32))

4. **Clean references get a floor.**
   Catalogue, inventory, and measurement lines with positive reference evidence and no detected character damage can be lifted from `Trash` to `Noisy`. The rule is explicitly a floor, not a demotion rule. ([github.com](https://github.com/ufal/atrium-alto-postprocess/pull/32))

5. **Several narrowly measured patterns now force `Trash`.**
   These include solitary letters, repeated alternating bigrams such as `IDIDIDID...`, and heavily fragmented lines with very short mean token length. ([github.com](https://github.com/ufal/atrium-alto-postprocess/pull/32))

6. **The pre-filter no longer corrupts numbers.**
   The `2 → z` OCR repair is prevented from firing inside numeric measurements and codes, avoiding transformations such as `0.25 → 0.z5` and `1932b → 193zb`. ([github.com](https://github.com/ufal/atrium-alto-postprocess/pull/32))

## Reported evaluation results

The PR reports three manually annotated validation samples:

| Sample | Lines evaluated | Previous `test` | PR #32 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Uniform random corpus sample | 300 | 61.3% | 79.0% | **+17.7 pp** |
| 16 complete documents | 170 categorized lines | 71.2% | 89.4% | **+18.2 pp** |
| 13 independent CTX documents | 654 categorized lines | 64.7% | 80.3% | **+15.6 pp** |

For the representative 300-line sample, the paired difference had a reported 95% confidence interval of 11.6–23.7 percentage points and an exact McNemar p-value of `5.2 × 10⁻⁹`. Among the 95 disagreements, the annotator preferred the new algorithm 69 times and the prior algorithm 16 times. A full-corpus re-score reportedly processed 47,677 documents and 40.5 million lines without runtime errors. ([github.com](https://github.com/ufal/atrium-alto-postprocess/pull/32))

However, the improvement has a substantial and clearly documented cost:

| “Needs attention” metric | Previous `test` | PR #32 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Recall | 72.2% | **46.3%** |
| Precision | 30.7% | **50.0%** |
| False alarms per 300 lines | 88 | **25** |
| Clear F1 | 0.75 | **0.89** |
| Trash F1 | 0.37 | **0.34** |

Therefore:

- **For clean-text delivery**, the new version is probably preferable: it sharply reduces false alarms and releases many more correctly recognized lines.
- **For a manual-correction worklist**, it is less suitable without further work because it misses more actual problems. ([github.com](https://github.com/ufal/atrium-alto-postprocess/pull/32))

One review caveat is that the contributor disclosed that the implementation, comments, and measurements were produced with Claude Opus, while the human contribution consisted of manual annotation, acceptance decisions, and corpus-level review. That does not invalidate the results, but it strengthens the case for independently reproducing the evaluation and reviewing the shared scoring path. ## Recommended next change

### Priority 1: extract one shared per-line scoring function

The strongest fix is to stop reconstructing production behavior in three places. Create one pure function, conceptually:

```python
def score_line_from_frozen_signals(
    *,
    text: str,
    original_text: str,
    original_lang: str,
    orig_lang_score: float,
    perplexity: float,
    expected_langs: list[str],
    known_lang_bases: frozenset[str],
) -> dict:
    ...
```

It should own:

1. short-line perplexity capping;
2. remapped display language and display score;
3. trust-tier scoring confidence;
4. original-text density and vowel metrics;
5. all structural detectors;
6. `compute_quality_score`;
7. `categorize_line`;
8. reason flags and aligned quality score.

Then use that same function from:

- `classify_TEXT.py`;
- `tools/recategorize_from_csv.py`;
- `test_smoke.py`;
- `test_rotation_regression.py`;
- calibration tests.

That would satisfy the parity test’s stated principle in reality, rather than merely sharing the final two functions.

### Priority 2: separate parity tests from accuracy tests

These are different contracts.

#### Engine parity fixture

**Purpose**: Given frozen model outputs and the same algorithm/config version, offline re-scoring must exactly reproduce production.

Recommended assertion:

```python
assert predicted[DERIVED_COLUMNS].equals(stored[DERIVED_COLUMNS])
```

The fixture should be regenerated whenever the intended algorithm changes, with its generating commit/config recorded.

#### Manually labeled calibration fixture

**Purpose**: The algorithm must retain acceptable quality against human ground truth.

Track at least:

- overall accuracy;
- `Clear` precision and recall;
- combined `Noisy|Trash` recall;
- `Trash` precision and recall;
- false alarms per fixed sample size;
- results by line length, especially one- and two-token lines;
- results by document, collection, and language.

This prevents an overall accuracy improvement from hiding the substantial problem-recall reduction documented in PR #32.

### Priority 3: handle the behavioral tests honestly

I recommend the following immediate policy:

| Case | Immediate test treatment |
|---|---|
| Clean CTX title | Regenerate fixture; expect `Clear` |
| `pbqdnuwmoxszeyv!!` | Expect `Noisy`, or at minimum assert `cat != "Clear"` |
| `olie` | Keep as known desired behavior, but mark strict `xfail` linked to #23 |
| `oueussd` | Mark only this parameter as strict `xfail` linked to #23 |

For example:

```python
@pytest.mark.xfail(
    strict=True,
    reason="Known lexical ambiguity for structurally clean short alpha tokens; tracked in #23",
)
def test_short_gibberish_token_routed_to_trash():
    ...
```

Using `strict=True` is useful: once a lexical improvement makes the test pass, pytest reports an unexpected pass and forces removal of the marker.

I would **not** rewrite the `olie` and `oueussd` tests to claim that `Clear` is semantically correct. They are known garbage examples. Treat them as accepted technical debt, not newly correct labels.

## Longer-term algorithm direction

For the specific failures here, a full replacement model may not be necessary initially. A narrower lexical component could be evaluated only in this ambiguous branch:

```text
one or two tokens
+ alphabetic
+ structurally clean
+ no strong Czech/reference evidence
+ gibberish/fused/rotation suspicion
```

Possible signals include:

- Czech word-frequency or morphological-dictionary lookup;
- character n-gram language probability;
- compact learned word-likeness classifier;
- comparison of original and rotation/mirror transforms;
- calibrated fallback to `Noisy`, rather than immediately to `Trash`.

Any such rule should be measured against:

- short Czech words;
- surnames and place names;
- German and Slovak toponyms;
- inventory codes;
- abbreviations and sigla.

The PR already demonstrates why a broad regex is risky: proposed uppercase/damage expansions were rejected after corpus scans showed that they would affect very large numbers of legitimate codes and locality identifiers. ([github.com](https://github.com/ufal/atrium-alto-postprocess/pull/32))

---

## Final recommendation

The best next PR would:

1. extract a single shared production scoring function;
2. make the offline re-scorer use production-identical inputs;
3. replace the duplicated smoke/rotation implementations;
4. regenerate the parity fixture, including every derived column;
5. change the `pbq...` expectation to `Noisy` or “not Clear”;
6. preserve `olie` and `oueussd` as strict known failures linked to #23;
7. add a human-labeled metric gate that tracks both clean-text precision and problem-line recall.

That produces green CI **without pretending the remaining short-token false negatives are correct**, while retaining the empirically valuable parts of the calibration.

### Summary of what I found so far - by Sonnet 5 Extra

I dug into the four failures in `tests/test_recategorize_parity.py`. All four come from a single root cause: **one line in `data_samples/DOC_LINE_CATEG/CTX000000001.csv`** flips category on re-score.

**The line:** `CTX000000001`, page 1, line 1 — `"Výzkumná zpráva č. 1/2024 — Hradiště u Horní Mezí"` (a 9-word report title with an em-dash and a "č. 1/2024" reference number). Stored ground truth: **Noisy**. Current re-score: **Clear**.

**What's *not* the cause** (I verified these are unchanged/identical between the CSV-generation commit and HEAD):
- `compute_quality_score`'s formula and all its weight/threshold constants
- `compute_garbage_density` / `compute_vowel_ratio` (and `text` == `original_text` for this row, so the "use original_text" fix in the WIP commit is a no-op here)
- The "Two-tier Trust System" lang-score swap also computes to the same value (1.0) as the old `remap_lang` path for this row, since the line's language is the expected one (`ces`)

**What *is* the cause:** rule 6, `rule_lowppl_clear`, in `determine_category` (`text_util.py`):
```python
if ppl < LOWPPL_CLEAR_MAX and word_count >= 3:
    if valid_word_ratio < MOSTLY_READABLE_VALID_MIN: ... Noisy
    if damaged: ... Noisy
    return "Clear", "lowppl_clear"
```
For this line: `ppl=30.62 < 50`, `word_count=9`, `valid_word_ratio=1.0`, not damaged → short-circuits straight to **Clear**, before ever reaching `is_forgiven_headline`/`check_rescues()` further down. I confirmed via manual formula recomputation that this is a real short-circuit, not a numeric fluke.

Importantly, **this bug predates the "add failing test draft of fixes" commit** — I checked out the prior commit (`f6adc23`) in a worktree and the identical 4 tests fail there too, with the exact same row. So the WIP changes in the HEAD commit (the "Two-tier Trust System" and "original_text" fixes) were a genuine attempt at issue #30's alignment work, but they don't touch the actual bug — `rule_lowppl_clear` firing ahead of the headline/forgiveness logic.

One relevant wrinkle: `is_forgiven_headline` itself would *also* reject this line (it caps at `HEADLINE_MAX_WORDS = 8`, and this line has 9 words), so simply making rule 6 defer to `forgiven` wouldn't automatically fix it either — the headline-word-cap may need adjusting too, or `rule_lowppl_clear` needs its own guard (e.g., against em-dash-separated title/subtitle structure, or a document-position/heuristic signal) independent of `is_forgiven_headline`.

I was cut off before finishing the bisection to find exactly when `rule_lowppl_clear` (or its threshold `LOWPPL_CLEAR_MAX=50.0`) was introduced/loosened, and before proposing a concrete code fix. I also haven't yet cleaned up the substantial dead/commented-out code left in `tools/recategorize_from_csv.py` and `text_util.py` from the WIP iterations (multiple fully-duplicated commented-out versions of `_rescore_row`, `determine_category`, `categorize_line`).

**Next steps I'd take:** pin down when `LOWPPL_CLEAR_MAX`/`rule_lowppl_clear` was introduced, decide the correct fix (likely: gate rule 6 behind a headline/title heuristic, or raise `HEADLINE_MAX_WORDS` and route through `forgiven`, or add an em-dash/subtitle-structure check), verify it doesn't regress any of the 491 currently-passing tests, then clean up the leftover dead code in both files.

### 2 — Penalty cascade: a real architecture reversal

Before PR #32 (v0.15.4, 2026-06-15): *"pure threshold routing on `quality_score` drives the
category … + 3 structural overrides replaced the old ~150-line penalty cascade"* (per
`agent_dev_logs/DEVLOG.md`). PR #32 kept that design and added its four new pieces
(`_is_neutral_token`, short-line gate, `count_damaged_tokens`, `is_clean_reference`) as more
independent overrides in the same style.

The follow-up commits pulled the six pre-existing override rules out of `determine_category`
entirely and reimplemented them as scalar `qs` penalties applied inside `categorize_line`
*before* `determine_category` sees the score — `thresh_trash` and the six `check_rescues()`
call sites tied to those rules are gone from `determine_category`; `check_rescues()` is now
reached only from the QS-band routing at the bottom (`:1280`). Net effect: these six signals no
longer independently force `Trash` on their own — they only lower `qs`, and the *existing*
QS-band threshold (now fed a lower score) plus the PR's own rescue floors (reference floor,
forgiven-headline) decide the final outcome. This is not a full revert to the pre-June-15
cascade (the six penalties now interact with PR #32's rescue floors, which didn't exist in June),
but it is the same *style* of fix (cumulative subtraction) that the June pass explicitly
retired, applied on top of the newer design. Worth a deliberate decision, not just accepting
the fixture re-baseline as confirmation it's fine.

### 3 & 4 — Two ad-hoc patches

Both are single-purpose string/character checks rather than general rules, which cuts against
config-driven, allowlist-style detection used everywhere else in this file:
- **#3** (zero-alpha override) is narrow and low-risk — it only fires on lines with no letters
  at all, and only when every existing rescue (forgiven, reference, upright-Czech) has already
  declined to apply.
- **#4** (em-dash + digit) is broader than its one motivating case. It directly reverses the
  PR's own Testing-section argument that `"Výzkumná zpráva č. 1/2024 — Hradiště u Horní Mezí"`
  scoring `Clear` was a fixture *mislabel*, not a regression — the PR asked for an explicit
  choice between fixing the fixture or narrowing the change, and this is the "narrow" choice,
  but implemented as a literal substring+digit match rather than a rule scoped to what actually
  made that line problematic. It will also catch any other clean, correctly-read headline that
  happens to contain an em dash and a stray digit anywhere in the line.

## **Integration Strategy: PR \#32 & Issue \#30**

**Verdict: Good integration plan, but wrong amount of architecture.**
The safest path is to preserve PR \#32’s behavioral wins (the short-line and reference-token fixes), keep the categorization logic declarative, and turn the two contentious changes into narrowly scoped, well-tested exceptions rather than a new scoring philosophy.

### **🟢 The Good: Preserving the Shape**

Separating the risky changes into their own commits with paired positive/negative regression tests is exactly the right way to handle this. It avoids "it passed CI, therefore it must be fine" thinking while preserving the behavioral improvements.

### **🔴 Design Concern: Item \#2 (The Penalty Cascade)**

Reinstating a cumulative penalty cascade is a step backward for the architecture. We must reject any "Degradation Vector" or cumulative subtraction models, as they add an unnecessary abstraction layer to a system expressly designed to be explicit.
**Actionable Path:** Restore the rules as explicit named decisions (gates) directly inside determine\_category(), complete with clear reason strings. This preserves the repository's documented gate model and keeps each rule auditable as its own decision point.

### **🔴 Scope Concern: Item \#4 (Em-dash \+ Digit Rule)**

A raw " — " \+ digit heuristic is a patch, not a principled rule. A broad substring check overreaches the stated intent of making narrow corrections.
**Actionable Path:** First, determine if the motivating example is actually a fixture mistake. If so, fix the fixture and add one regression test. If a rule is genuinely needed, replace the broad matcher with a tightly scoped predicate tied to the actual failure mode.

#### 🔍 Root Cause Analysis of Regressions

The regressions stem directly from stacking two highly restrictive policies in the same pass:

1. **The `damaged` Backstop Overreach:** Restoring the character-level damage check as a final, absolute cap to `Noisy` is too blunt. It traps highly readable lines that contain a single stray typographic symbol (e.g., `Nádoba 2* Zvoncový pohár se zakulaceným, uprostř` demoted purely due to the `*`).
2. **Hard Gates vs. Soft Penalties:** Restoring the explicit hard gates removed the `-0.35` quality score buffer. Previously, minor spacing issues or bigrams (e.g., `K ct.dopisu Vašemu...` or `Při nich ani kolem nebyla žádná nádoba,neb střep`) took a penalty but survived in `Clear` due to high language confidence and valid word ratios. 1. **Ablate the `damaged` backstop:** Run a pass explicitly bypassing the `damaged` token cap to quantify exactly how much of the `Clear -> Noisy` bleed it is responsible for.

## A defect in the new predicate

**`\b` after an escaped dot cannot match.** In

```python
full_measurement_keywords = r"\b(?:rozměry?|rozm\.|výška|…)\b"
descriptor_keywords       = r"\b(?:pr\.|hl\.|dna|hrdla|okraje)\b"
```

the trailing `\b` sits after `\.`. A dot is a non-word character, so a boundary
exists there only if the *next* character is a word character — the entry can
never match when the abbreviation is followed by a space or ends the line:

| line | `_looks_like_measurement` |
|---|---|
| `pr.okraje - 145` | `True` |
| `pr. okraje - 145` | **`False`** |
| `rozm.12` | `True` |
| `rozm. 12` | **`False`** |
| `pr. dna - 7` | **`False`** |

The last row is the example given in the comment directly above
`has_measurement_separator`. Whether a line is protected now depends on whether
OCR emitted a space after the dot. Dropping the trailing `\b` on the dot-final
entries fixes it; the leading `\b` already does the intended work.

One thing that may save you time: of the demotions the Gemini report lists in
`CTX193001369`, L52, L72, L86 and L96 keep `is_structured_line` in **both**
commits — they carry `mm` units, so the predicate never stops protecting them.
Their demotions come from elsewhere in the change. Only L39 loses the
predicate. Tuning `_looks_like_measurement` will not recover that file.

## On the single-letter unit: withdrawing my own suggestion

Restricting units to `mm cm km kg ml ha` was what I proposed last time, and
having measured it I withdraw it. In `is_clean_reference` a single-letter unit
*lifted* garbage (`3 L`, `11 g`) out of Trash, which is why PR #32 dropped it

Removing `m` therefore withdraws protection from the largest class, not the
smallest. I carried the conclusion across from `is_clean_reference` without

#### By Claude Opus 5 — measurement

Base: `upstream/test` 03a8bd4. `test` has since moved to 0b4987e (v1.4.4-beta), but `text_util.py` is byte-identical across those six commits, so every number below applies to the current head unchanged; the commit also cherry-picks onto it without a conflict. ## 1. What the rule does today

```python
if "rule_short_garbage" not in DISABLED_RULES and not forgiven and not structured:
    if (
        word_count <= ISOLATED_CHAR_MIN_TOKENS
        and not has_cz_diacs(text_source)
        and (lang_score <= LANG_SCORE_REMAP or rot_ratio >= SUSPICIOUS_ROT_RATIO)
        and (gibberish_present or weird_ratio > 0.0)
    ):
        _fire("rule_short_garbage")
        return "Trash", "trash_threshold"     # unconditional, no check_rescues()
```

[Current `test`, lines 1165–1173](https://github.com/ufal/atrium-alto-postprocess/blob/0b4987e/text_util.py#L1165-L1173); the trailing comment is mine.

It is the single largest Trash producer in the sample: **6 333 firings = 28.4 %** of all Trash lines (62.5 % on the two documents Dana sent).

## 2. How much the two narrowing clauses actually narrow

Measured over all 6 333 firings:

| clause | holds in |
|---|---|
| `lang_score <= LANG_SCORE_REMAP` (0.75) | 6 128 = **96.8 %** |
| — of which capped exactly by the trust tier | 5 545 = 87.6 % |
| `weird_ratio > 0.0` | 6 252 = **98.7 %** |

The language clause is close to always true by construction: `lang_score` is already multiplied by the tier of trust, and anything outside ces/deu/eng/fra/pol/ita is multiplied by **0.50**, so the ceiling is 0.50 < 0.75. On a one-token line fastText is guessing anyway — `malakofauna` comes back as Icelandic, `diapozitiv` as Romanian, `Nivelacc` as Vietnamese.

The `weird_ratio > 0.0` bar admits any non-zero weirdness.

What is left after both is: **1–3 tokens without Czech diacritics → Trash.**

## 3. The diacritics asymmetry

`has_cz_diacs()` is the only escape. Same words, same perplexity, same language score, one accented character appended:

| input | result | | input | result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `malakofauna` | **Trash** | | `malakofaunaá` | Clear |
| `diapozitiv` | **Trash** | | `diapozitivá` | Clear |
| `kontext` | **Trash** | | `kontextá` | Clear |
| `textil` | **Trash** | | `textilá` | Clear |

So it does not protect Czech words, it protects words that happen to carry diacritics. Structurally unprotected: Latin binomials (`Equus caballus`, `Canis familiaris`, `Pinus silvestris`), osteology (`Phalanx proximalis`, `Maxilla+dentes`, `Occipitale`), German, proper names, context codes, URLs, and plain diacritic-free Czech (`kontext`, `slepenec`, `hr. komponenta 4`).

|---|---|---|---|
| 1960 | 4 793 | 104 | 2.2 % |
| 1970 | 7 501 | 433 | 5.8 % |

## 5. The change

One hunk: route the rule through the same gate as its six siblings instead of returning unconditionally.

```diff
             _fire("rule_short_garbage")
-            return "Trash", "trash_threshold"
+            if qs < CATEG_TRASH_SCORE_MAX + 0.35 and _has_strong_garbage_evidence(
+                text_source, valid_word_ratio=valid_word_ratio, lang_score=lang_score,
+                orig_lang_score=orig_lang_score, gibberish_present=gibberish_present,
+                garbage_density=garbage_density, weird_ratio=weird_ratio,
+                is_upright_czech=is_upright_czech,
+            ):
+                return "Trash", "trash_threshold"
```

The `qs` part is measurably inert here — a variant without it produces identical output on the test documents; the evidence gate does all the work. I kept it only for symmetry with the other six rules, and it is the first thing to drop if you prefer the shorter form.

## 7. What it costs

**One test fails:** `test_rotation_regression.py::test_inverted_trash_stays_trash_at_default[oueussd…]`. This is the honest weak point. `_has_strong_garbage_evidence()` cannot tell `oueussd` from `malakofauna` — both are single diacritic-free tokens with `valid_word_ratio` 1.0 and a high nominal language score. No character-level rule we tried separates them; it needs a lexicon or phonotactics. So this is a real trade, not a free win, and the `oueussd` case is the price. Either the test is adjusted, or the rule is narrowed some other way — I have no good answer yet and would welcome yours.

To be precise about what that costs on your board: `pytest -m "not slow"` on clean 0b4987e is green (670 tests, 0 failures, checked today), so with this patch that failure would be the only red mark, not one of several.

**Precision of the lifts.** 508 of the changed lines, drawn from both collections and stratified by decade, were graded by a human annotator:

| | share | examples |
|---|---|---|
| correctly lifted | 69.7 % | `Equus caballus`, `Canis familiaris`, `Occipitale`, `hr. komponenta 2`, `Kontext` |
| borderline | 15.4 % | `bquus ferus t.caballus`, `Uniocrassus`, `2 .tvrz.`, `Ji r` |
| **wrongly lifted** | **15.0 %** | `vansasaasasa`, `Tthts I`, `rragment`, `vfetennl k.` |

That is **4.7 : 1** improved to harmed, or 2.3 : 1 if borderline counts as harm.

The sample deliberately over-weights pre-2000 documents, which are the harder ones, so those raw shares are pessimistic for the corpus. Re-weighted to how the changes are actually distributed, it is **75.6 % / 8.5 % / 16.0 % (4.7 : 1)** for ARÚP and **78.3 % / 10.5 % / 11.2 % (7.0 : 1)** for ARUB. An earlier, smaller 260-line blind grading on ARÚP alone gave 78 / 8 / 14 — the same picture within sampling noise, which is the main reason I trust these figures at all.

Two patterns in the grading are worth passing on, because they are the obvious places to attack if you want the error rate lower:

| tokens on the line | graded | correct : wrong |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 167 | 3.6 : 1 |
| 2 | 220 | 5.1 : 1 |
| 3 | 114 | 6.8 : 1 |

The table covers 501 of the 508 graded lines. The other seven have four or more tokens, which the rule cannot fire on: they moved through `pp_page_context`. Once the rule stops trashing short lines, a page's garbage ratio falls and the page-context pass no longer sweeps its neighbours. All seven carry that flag and all seven were lifted Trash→Noisy, so the change does reach beyond `word_count <= 3` indirectly — at 1.4 % of the graded sample, though, that is not the bulk of what it does.

Shorter is riskier, monotonically. I tried the obvious narrowing — leave `word_count == 1` alone — and measured it against the same grading: it lifts the ratio to 5.4 : 1 but discards a third of the correct lifts to remove 42 % of the errors, and single-token lines are exactly what was reported as the original complaint. I did not pursue it, but the numbers are there if you disagree.

By decade, the oldest scans are where it goes wrong most: in ARÚP the 1930s grade 15 correct against 12 wrong, and the 1920s 11 against 8, while the 1950s and 1960s run 25:2 and 23:1. Those two worst decades are 1 108 lines out of 197 218, so this does not move the total much — but if this ships, that is the population to look at first.

**Counter-evidence, from our own older annotations.** We have 1 567 lines annotated during earlier calibration rounds; 1 560 of them can be matched line for line in both recomputations (every document is present, seven individual lines could not be located by page and line index). Baseline agrees with the annotation on 79.2 % of them; with the patch, 78.8 %. The patch moves 9 of those 1 560 lines, and **not one of them lands on the annotated category.** Six move away from it — `NINNNIC`, `Slaot-o hiezazzt`, `Ch. i6dn.283/54`, `p.nA o.` and `cuxoaid ,` on two separate lines — the same phonotactic-garbage class as `oueussd`. Of the remaining three, two move partway toward the annotation (`sektor B` and `0,2-0,4 m`, both annotated Clear, lifted Trash→Noisy) and one partway away (`IDIDIDIDIDIDUOID`, annotated Non-text).

I do not think this cancels the grading above — those sets were built by sampling disputed lines from earlier rounds, so they are enriched for garbage and barely overlap this rule's population: only 73 of the 1 560 are Trash in the baseline at all. But it is the sharpest thing we have against the patch, it points at exactly the weakness at the top of this section, and it is the first thing I would want to see if I were reviewing this.

Her assessment, in summary: on pre-2000 documents the change is barely visible, on post-2000 documents the improvement is substantial, and while she did see cases where a Trash line is lifted to Clear or Noisy wrongly, she is in favour of adopting this version.
